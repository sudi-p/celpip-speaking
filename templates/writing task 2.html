What the task involves

You are asked to write a persuasive essay (180–199 words) taking a clear position on a controversial topic. You have 26 minutes to plan, write, and revise.

The examiner is testing your ability to develop an argument, support claims with evidence, organize ideas logically, and demonstrate sophisticated grammar and vocabulary in an academic context.

What examiners score you on

Task Fulfillment

Did you take a clear position? Is it 180-199 words? Did you support your argument with evidence?

Coherence

Is the essay organized logically? Do ideas flow from intro to conclusion with clear transitions?

Vocabulary

Academic register, varied word choice, and sophisticated transitions.

Grammar

Accurate sentence structure, varied syntax, and minimal errors.

The 5-paragraph structure

1

Introduction (2–3 sentences)

Hook, context, and clear thesis statement taking a position

2,3,4

Body Paragraphs (3 × 3–4 sentences)

Each supports the thesis with one main idea, evidence, and reasoning

5

Conclusion (2–3 sentences)

Restate thesis, summarize key points, end with a strong final thought

Survey-Based Persuasive Essay: Choose ONE option (A or B), state your position clearly, and support with 2–3 strong reasons. Use the powerful "Admittedly...However..." strategy for advanced scoring.

Hook the reader, provide context, and state your thesis clearly. Your thesis is your main argument.

  • ✦Start with a hook: A question, statement, or statistic that grabs attention. "Should social media be regulated by governments?"
  • ✦Provide background: 1–2 sentences explaining the issue and why it matters.
  • ✦State your thesis: "I believe that social media platforms should be held legally accountable for user data privacy."

Example intro

"In today's digital world, social media companies collect vast amounts of personal data. This raises serious concerns about privacy and data security. I believe that governments must implement stricter regulations to protect users' personal information."

State your reason, explain the benefit, and show the extended result. Use a strong topic sentence that directly supports your choice.

Body paragraph formula:

• Reason: "First, [your choice] provides [specific benefit]."

• Specific example: Concrete detail showing what/how/why

• Extended result: "As a result..." / "This means..." / "Consequently..."

  • ✦Be specific: Instead of "it's good," say "it improves X by addressing Y, which results in Z"
  • ✦Use varied vocabulary: Avoid repeating words from the prompt
  • ✦Use transitions: "First," "A key advantage is," "The primary benefit..."

Example body paragraph (survey response)

"First, choosing Option A promotes professional development. Specifically, this approach equips employees with advanced skills that directly enhance their job performance. As a result, workers become more confident, productive, and better able to contribute to company success."

The "Admittedly...However" strategy is a HALLMARK of high-scoring essays. Acknowledge a valid point about the other option, then explain why YOUR choice is still superior.

Admittedly...However formula:

• Admittedly, [concede a good point about Option B]

• However, [explain why Option A is still better] because [specific reason]

• This shows balanced thinking and sophisticated reasoning!

  • ✦Shows critical thinking: You acknowledge the other side's validity
  • ✦Strengthens your argument: You explain WHY your choice wins despite the tradeoff
  • ✦Sophisticated language: Demonstrates academic maturity and control

Example with "Admittedly...However"

"Admittedly, Option B would reduce initial costs and simplify planning. However, Option A offers superior long-term value because it addresses the core community need more effectively. While the upfront investment is higher, the lasting benefits far outweigh the temporary expense."

Present your final argument or strongest reason. This is your last persuasive moment before concluding.

Transition phrases to start this paragraph:

• "Most importantly..." / "Ultimately..." / "Perhaps most significantly..."

• "Additionally..." / "Furthermore..." / "Moreover..."

• "Finally..." / "In conclusion..." (but save full conclusion for next section)

  • ✦Save strongest argument for last: End on a powerful note
  • ✦Use varied sentence structures: Mix simple and complex sentences
  • ✦Lead into conclusion: This paragraph should naturally transition to your final restatement

Example final reason paragraph

"Most importantly, Option A creates lasting community value that benefits multiple generations. Unlike short-term solutions, this approach builds infrastructure that strengthens our neighborhood's long-term viability. This enduring benefit makes it the clear choice for sustainable progress."

Restate your thesis, summarize key points, and leave the reader with a strong final thought.

  • ✦Restate thesis (in new words): Don't repeat verbatim — rephrase your main argument.
  • ✦Briefly summarize: Mention your 3 main points in 1–2 sentences.
  • ✦Final thought: End with impact. "In a world where privacy is increasingly threatened, legal action is not just necessary—it is urgent."

Example conclusion

"In conclusion, strict government regulation of social media platforms is essential for protecting user privacy, preventing data breaches, and ensuring corporate accountability. The evidence clearly shows that self-regulation has failed. As technology continues to advance, legal frameworks must evolve to protect citizens' fundamental right to privacy."

Essay 1: Remote vs. Office Work

"I strongly believe remote work is the superior choice for modern businesses. First, remote work significantly increases productivity by eliminating commute stress and office distractions. Employees working from home report higher focus and substantially better output quality. As a result, companies benefit from reduced turnover and increased overall efficiency. Admittedly, office collaboration provides valuable in-person interaction. However, modern technology like video conferencing and project management tools effectively replicate this benefit without the downside of daily commuting costs and time loss. Furthermore, remote work reduces environmental impact and cuts transportation costs for both employees and employers, thereby contributing to sustainability goals. Most importantly, remote work improves employee well-being by offering flexibility and better work-life balance. In conclusion, remote work policies benefit productivity, environmental responsibility, and employee satisfaction. Companies should prioritize this flexible approach for sustainable long-term success and competitive advantage."

Essay 2: University vs. Practical Training

"I believe practical training programs are more valuable for career success in today's competitive job market. First, practical training equips students with immediately applicable skills employers actively demand. Graduates can begin contributing to their careers faster than university graduates. As a result, they earn income and build professional experience considerably sooner. Admittedly, university education provides theoretical knowledge and broader career possibilities. However, this broader knowledge often comes at excessive cost and time investment without guaranteed job placement, unlike specialized technical training programs. Furthermore, practical training costs significantly less than university tuition, reducing financial burden substantially. Most importantly, practical training creates direct pathways to employment through established industry connections. In conclusion, practical training offers superior value through immediate employment readiness, affordability, and direct career application. It represents the smarter choice for most students seeking financial security and rapid career advancement."

Essay 3: Company Uniforms

"I strongly support implementing company uniforms in workplace settings. First, uniforms promote professionalism and create a cohesive company image. When clients visit, they immediately recognize employees and perceive the organization as organized and serious. This strengthens client confidence and trust substantially. Additionally, uniforms foster equality and team spirit by removing fashion-based comparisons among staff members. This creates an inclusive work environment where people are evaluated by performance, not clothing choices. As a result, team productivity and morale improve significantly. Admittedly, some employees value personal expression through fashion. However, workplace professionalism and team cohesion outweigh individual wardrobe preferences. Furthermore, uniforms reduce daily stress by eliminating the burden of choosing work outfits, allowing employees to focus entirely on job responsibilities. In conclusion, company uniforms represent a practical investment in professionalism, equality, and employee well-being that benefits organizations substantially while enhancing workplace culture."

Essay 4: Public Swimming Pool vs. Community Garden

"I believe the community garden is the better investment for our diverse neighborhood. First, a garden serves all community members year-round regardless of age or swimming ability. Unlike pools, gardens benefit the elderly, young children, and non-swimmers equally through fresh produce access and recreational activity. This inclusivity creates meaningful community engagement. Admittedly, pools provide excellent recreational exercise opportunities for swimmers. However, pools require continuous expensive maintenance, operate seasonally, and serve limited populations. Gardens operate continuously and improve food security for everyone. Furthermore, community gardens strengthen neighborhood bonds by bringing diverse residents together around shared cultivation activities. This generates stronger social cohesion than pools typically create. Most importantly, gardens address food insecurity and promote environmental sustainability through urban agriculture, contributing to long-term neighborhood well-being. In conclusion, the community garden better serves our neighborhood's diverse needs, promotes sustainability, and builds community connections that benefit residents profoundly long-term."

Starting Body Paragraphs:

  • • First, [option] provides...
  • • A key advantage is...
  • • The primary benefit...
  • • Most importantly...
  • • Furthermore... / Additionally...

Explaining Results (CRITICAL!):

  • • As a result... / Consequently...
  • • This means... / This leads to...
  • • Therefore... / Thus...
  • • The outcome is... / This benefits...
  • • Ultimately... / In the long term...

The Powerful Concession (9+ scores):

  • • Admittedly... / Certainly... / True,
  • • One could argue that...
  • • While [option B] does offer...
  • • However... / Yet... / Still...
  • • Despite this... / Nonetheless...

Closing & Emphasis:

  • • In conclusion... / To summarize...
  • • Ultimately... / In the end...
  • • Therefore... / For these reasons...
  • • This clearly demonstrates...
  • • The evidence strongly supports...

Persuasive Verbs (not "is/has"):

  • • Ensures / Promotes / Fosters
  • • Enhance / Strengthen / Improve
  • • Demonstrates / Shows / Reveals
  • • Creates / Generates / Establishes
  • • Provides / Offers / Delivers

Avoid (Too Weak/Casual):

  • • "I think" / "I believe" (once in thesis)
  • • "really" / "very" / "definitely"
  • • "a lot" / "lots of" (use specific terms)
  • • "nice" / "good" (too vague)
  • • Contractions: "don't," "isn't," "can't"

How examiners score Task 2 on a 12-point scale. The bands below show typical essay characteristics at each level.

Score 10–12 (Advanced)

●

Task Fulfillment: Clear position taken. Word count 180–199. Well-developed argument with compelling evidence. Completed within time constraints.

Coherence: Essay flows logically. Strong paragraph structure and transitions. Ideas build toward a conclusion.

Vocabulary: Academic register throughout. Sophisticated transitions and persuasive language. Varied word choice.

Grammar: Grammar and spelling are nearly perfect. Varied sentence structures demonstrate control.

Score 7–9 (Upper-Intermediate)

●

Task Fulfillment: Position is clear. Word count generally within range. Argument is supported but could be stronger.

Coherence: Essay is organized and easy to follow. Most paragraphs have clear topic sentences. Some transitions present.

Vocabulary: Generally academic tone. Some variety in word choice. Occasional repetition of key terms.

Grammar: Grammar is mostly accurate. Minor errors present but don't disrupt meaning.

Score 4–6 (Intermediate)

●

Task Fulfillment: Position exists but may be unclear. Word count may be below range. Argument is basic and underdeveloped.

Coherence: Essay structure exists but may lack clear organization. Transitions are basic or missing.

Vocabulary: Some academic vocabulary but often basic. Limited variety. Repetitive word choice.

Grammar: Several grammatical errors. Some spelling mistakes. Meaning is usually clear but sometimes unclear.

Score 1–3 (Below Intermediate)

●

Task Fulfillment: Position unclear or missing. Word count significantly below range. Minimal development of argument.

Coherence: Organization is weak or absent. Difficult to follow. No clear paragraph structure.

Vocabulary: Very basic vocabulary. Not academic in register. Heavy repetition.

Grammar: Frequent and serious errors. Meaning is sometimes unclear.

Key insight

A 10–12 essay takes a clear position, develops it with three distinct arguments, and uses sophisticated language. It is well-organized and nearly error-free. It demonstrates persuasive writing with logical structure, good command of English, and efficient use of the 26-minute time frame. It is not perfect, and that's fine — what matters is that you deliver quality ideas concisely.

Planning

Spend 5 minutes planning before you write.

Write your thesis and outline your 3 main arguments. This prevents rambling and ensures a strong structure.

Planning

Take a clear position from the start.

Don't sit on the fence. "I believe X is better than Y" is stronger than "Both have advantages and disadvantages."

Structure

Use the 5-paragraph format consistently.

Introduction → 3 body paragraphs → Conclusion. This is expected and immediately shows organization.

Structure

Start each body paragraph with a topic sentence.

"First, X is important because..." — this tells the reader what to expect in that paragraph.

Language

Use transition phrases to connect ideas.

"Furthermore," "In contrast," "As a result" make your essay flow better and show advanced writing.

Language

Vary your sentence structure.

Mix short sentences with longer, complex ones. This shows grammatical control and keeps the essay engaging.

Mistakes to Avoid

Don't exceed the word count too much.

180–199 is the target. If you write 250+ words, it signals poor time management and inability to concisely organize your thoughts within the 26-minute window.

Mistakes to Avoid

Don't use "I think" or "In my opinion" constantly.

You can use it in your thesis, but avoid repeating it. "It is clear that..." or "Evidence shows..." sound more academic.

Mistakes to Avoid

Proofread before submitting.

You have no time limit — spend the last 5 minutes checking spelling, grammar, and punctuation. Typos are avoidable.
