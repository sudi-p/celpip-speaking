What the task involves

You are shown a photograph and asked to describe what you see in detail. There is no question — just an instruction like "Describe the image in as much detail as possible."

The examiner is testing your ability to organize ideas, use descriptive vocabulary, apply spatial language, and speak fluently without long pauses.

Scoring criteria (what examiners watch for)
Vocabulary Range and precision of words used to describe what you see
Coherence Logical flow from one detail to the next — not jumping around
Fluency Smooth delivery with minimal hesitation or filler sounds
Accuracy Grammar, tense consistency (present continuous for actions), and sentence variety

A 3-part structure that keeps your answer organized and natural — not a script to memorize word-for-word.

Pro tips
✦ Always use present continuous for ongoing actions: "A woman is carrying..."
✦ Move your eye across the image like scanning a scene — foreground → middle → background
✦ Speculate when unsure: "It appears to be..." / "This might be..." — shows advanced vocabulary
✦ Never say "I see…" repeatedly — use the transition phrases in the vocab bank instead
✦ Add inference sentences (what you think is happening) to fill time confidently
