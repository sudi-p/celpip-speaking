Writing · Task 1 of 2

Write a professional email

Compose a clear, courteous email responding to a business or personal situation. Focus on clarity, tone, and appropriate formatting.

Word count
150–200
Time limit
None
Score scale
1–12
Target band
8–10

What the task involves

You are given a situation and asked to write an email in response. For example: "Your colleague is sick and you need to email your manager about who will cover the presentation."

The examiner is testing your ability to communicate professionally, use appropriate tone and register, organize ideas clearly, and follow email conventions.

What examiners score you on

Task fulfillment

Responding appropriately to all parts of the prompt. Address the situation fully.

Coherence

Ideas organized logically. Email flows naturally from greeting to closing.

Vocabulary

Precise word choice. Professional tone with appropriate formality level.

Grammar

Accurate spelling, punctuation, and sentence structures. Few or no errors.

A 4-part structure for clear, professional email writing.

Choose a greeting based on your relationship to the recipient and context.

Options

"Dear [Name]," (formal), "Hi [Name]," (neutral), "Hello [Name]," (friendly but professional)

  • ✦Use the person's full name or title if formal
  • ✦If you don't know the recipient, use "Dear Sir or Madam,"
  • ✦Always follow with a comma or colon

The first sentence should make the purpose of your email immediately clear.

Template

"I am writing to [request / inform / ask / propose]..."

Example

"I am writing to request a meeting with you to discuss the upcoming project timeline."

  • ✦Be direct and specific about your purpose
  • ✦Keep to 1–2 sentences maximum
  • ✦Avoid vague language or unnecessary details

Develop your ideas clearly with relevant information, explanations, or requests. Use short paragraphs for readability.

Structure

Paragraph 1: Background/context. Paragraph 2: Main point/request. Paragraph 3: Additional details or next steps (if needed).

  • ✦Use transition words: "Furthermore," "Additionally," "As a result"
  • ✦Keep paragraphs short (3–5 sentences each)
  • ✦Maintain professional tone throughout

End with a professional closing that matches your opening formality level.

Options

"Sincerely," (formal), "Best regards," (neutral), "Thank you," (warm but professional)

  • ✦Closing should match your greeting's formality
  • ✦Always sign with your full name on a new line
  • ✦Include contact details if appropriate

Could you please

Polite request. "Could you please provide the report by Friday?"

I would appreciate it if

Formal request. "I would appreciate it if you could attend the meeting."

Furthermore

Adds information. "Furthermore, we should consider the budget."

As a result

Shows consequence. "As a result, we need to extend the deadline."

With regard to

Formal reference. "With regard to your proposal, I have some questions."

At your earliest convenience

Formal timing. "Please respond at your earliest convenience."

Looking forward to hearing from you

Professional closing phrase. Works well before sign-off.

Please let me know if you have any questions

Invitation for follow-up. Helpful closing sentence.

Would you be available to

Professional inquiry. "Would you be available to discuss this further?"

I am writing to confirm

Formal opening. "I am writing to confirm receipt of your email."

Five email writing scenarios. Choose one and practice writing a professional response within 150–200 words.

Scenario 1

Your manager asks for an update on a project you're working on.

Write an email summarizing your progress, any challenges, and your expected completion date.

Scenario 2

You want to request time off for a personal matter.

Email your supervisor explaining your request, proposing dates, and addressing how work will be covered.

Scenario 3

You received incorrect information in a document and need to point it out politely.

Write an email that diplomatically raises the issue and suggests a correction.

Scenario 4

A client thanks you for helping with a problem and you want to respond professionally.

Compose a follow-up email that acknowledges their thanks and offers continued support.

Scenario 5

You cannot attend an important meeting and need to notify participants.

Email the meeting organizer explaining your absence and suggesting alternatives.

How examiners score Writing Task 1. Clear communication and proper email format are critical.

Score 10–12 (Advanced)

●

Task fulfillment: Fully addresses all aspects of the prompt with appropriate detail and tone.

Coherence: Ideas flow logically. Email is well-organized with clear connections between paragraphs.

Vocabulary: Precise, varied word choice. Appropriate register for context. Professional language throughout.

Grammar: Few or no errors. Correct punctuation, spelling, and sentence structure. Proper email formatting.

Score 7–9 (Upper-Intermediate)

●

Task fulfillment: Addresses the prompt adequately with mostly relevant content.

Coherence: Generally organized. Ideas are mostly clear; some transitions could be smoother.

Vocabulary: Good range of vocabulary. Mostly appropriate register with occasional lapses in formality.

Grammar: Minor errors present but don't impede understanding. Generally follows email format correctly.

Score 4–6 (Intermediate)

●

Task fulfillment: Addresses main point but may miss some aspects or include irrelevant details.

Coherence: Basic organization present. Some ideas may be unclear or loosely connected.

Vocabulary: Limited range. Repetition is noticeable. Tone may be inappropriately informal or formal.

Grammar: Several errors present, some affecting clarity. Formatting may be incomplete.

Score 1–3 (Below Intermediate)

●

Task fulfillment: Does not adequately address the prompt. May be incomplete or off-topic.

Coherence: Ideas are disorganized. Difficult to follow. Poor paragraph structure.

Vocabulary: Very limited range. Inappropriate register for most of the email.

Grammar: Frequent errors that often obscure meaning. Poor formatting.

Key insight

A high-scoring email is clear, concise, and professional. It directly addresses the prompt, uses appropriate tone and vocabulary, and follows standard email conventions. Stick to 150–200 words — brevity shows control.

Structure

Follow the 4-part formula: Greeting → Opening → Body → Closing.

A clear structure makes your email easier to understand and shows you can organize ideas logically.

Tone

Match your tone to your relationship with the recipient.

A formal greeting with a casual body, or vice versa, will confuse the reader and lower your score.

Language

Use transition words to connect ideas.

"Furthermore," "In addition," "As a result," and "However" show logical thinking and improve coherence.

Language

Use active voice when possible.

"I will send the report" (active) is better than "The report will be sent by me" (passive).

Mistakes

Don't write like text messaging.

Avoid abbreviations ("ur," "asap," "thx") and emojis. Professional writing requires full words and standard punctuation.

Mistakes

Don't ramble or exceed word count.

150–200 words is the target. Too much content suggests poor editing; too little may not fully address the prompt.

Structure

Use short paragraphs (3–5 sentences max).

Shorter paragraphs are easier to read and show you can break down your ideas logically.

Tone

Proofread before submitting.

Spelling and grammar errors hurt your score. Read your email aloud to catch mistakes and awkward phrasing.

Professional email writing requires clarity, appropriate tone, and proper formatting. Master the 4-part structure, choose your words carefully, and always proofread. A well-written email demonstrates command of English in a real-world context.
