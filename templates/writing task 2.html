Writing · Task 2 of 2

Write a persuasive essay

Compose an essay arguing a position on a topic or responding to a prompt. Demonstrate structured thinking, strong evidence, and clear persuasion.

Word count
200–250
Time limit
None
Score scale
1–12
Target band
8–10

What the task involves

You are given a topic or statement and asked to write an essay taking a position on it. For example: "Does technology help or harm modern education? Write an essay arguing your position."

The examiner is testing your ability to structure an argument, support claims with evidence, organize paragraphs logically, and demonstrate control of complex English.

What examiners score you on

Task fulfillment

Taking a clear position and developing it throughout the essay. Fully addressing the prompt.

Coherence

Clear introduction, body paragraphs with topic sentences, and conclusion. Logical argument progression.

Vocabulary

Advanced vocabulary. Words that express opinion, reasoning, and persuasion. Varied word choice.

Grammar

Accurate complex sentences. Varied tenses. Few or no errors that impede meaning.

Essay structure

1

Introduction

Hook, context, and clear thesis statement

2

Body (2–3 paragraphs)

Topic sentence, evidence, explanation for each point

3

Conclusion

Restate thesis and summarize key points

A 5-paragraph essay formula for clear, persuasive writing.

Open with a hook that grabs attention, provide context, and end with a clear thesis that states your position.

Template

[Hook statement]. [Context/background]. Therefore, I believe [your thesis].

Example

"Many students struggle with traditional classroom learning. Increasingly, technology offers tools that personalize education and increase engagement. Therefore, I believe technology has significantly improved modern education."

  • ✦Hook with a question, statistic, or bold statement
  • ✦Thesis must be clear and arguable, not obvious
  • ✦Introduction should be 3–4 sentences

Each body paragraph should support your thesis with a single main idea, evidence, and explanation. Use transition words to connect paragraphs.

Template

[Topic sentence]. For example, [specific evidence/example]. This demonstrates [analysis of evidence].

Example

"First, technology enables personalized learning. For example, adaptive learning software adjusts difficulty based on each student's pace. This demonstrates how technology can meet individual student needs in ways traditional classrooms cannot."

  • ✦Begin each paragraph with a strong topic sentence
  • ✦Support claims with specific examples or evidence
  • ✦Analyze how your evidence supports your thesis

Restate your thesis in fresh language, briefly summarize your main points, and end with a broader reflection or call to action.

Template

In conclusion, [restate thesis]. As demonstrated, [brief summary of key points]. Therefore, [broader implication or recommendation].

Example

"In conclusion, technology has fundamentally transformed modern education for the better. As demonstrated, it enables personalized learning and increases student engagement. Therefore, educators should embrace technology as a tool to enhance, not replace, traditional instruction."

  • ✦Use closing phrases: "In conclusion," "To summarize," "In summary"
  • ✦Rephrase your thesis without simply repeating it
  • ✦End with a strong closing statement or recommendation

It is clear that

States a definitive point. "It is clear that technology benefits education."

One could argue that

Introduces a perspective. "One could argue that traditional methods are outdated."

For example

Introduces specific evidence. "For example, adaptive software personalizes learning."

This demonstrates that

Connects evidence to argument. "This demonstrates that engagement improves."

Furthermore

Adds another point. "Furthermore, technology reduces inequality."

In contrast

Presents opposing view. "In contrast, some argue technology distracts students."

In conclusion

Signals the end. "In conclusion, technology improves education."

To summarize

Recaps main ideas. "To summarize, the evidence supports this position."

Undoubtedly

Shows confidence. "Undoubtedly, this approach works best."

As a result

Shows consequence. "As a result, student performance improves."

Five essay prompts. Choose one and write an essay within 200–250 words using the 5-paragraph structure.

Prompt 1

Does remote work improve quality of life for employees?

Take a position and support it with evidence about flexibility, work-life balance, productivity, and isolation.

Prompt 2

Should universities focus more on practical skills or theoretical knowledge?

Argue your position using examples from career preparation, employability, and intellectual development.

Prompt 3

Is social media more beneficial or harmful to society?

Take a stance and support it with evidence about communication, mental health, information spread, and social connection.

Prompt 4

Should governments prioritize environmental protection over economic growth?

Develop a position using examples of climate change, business impact, sustainability, and future generations.

Prompt 5

Is it better to pursue a career you love or one that pays well?

Argue your position using evidence about fulfillment, financial security, long-term satisfaction, and life priorities.

How examiners score Writing Task 2. Argument structure and evidence quality are most important.

Score 10–12 (Advanced)

●

Task fulfillment: Clear thesis with strong supporting arguments. Fully addresses the prompt with depth.

Coherence: Well-organized with smooth transitions between ideas. Clear essay structure evident throughout.

Vocabulary: Sophisticated, varied vocabulary. Words precisely chosen to strengthen argument.

Grammar: Complex sentences used confidently with minimal errors. Demonstrates control of English.

Score 7–9 (Upper-Intermediate)

●

Task fulfillment: Clear position with adequate supporting points. Addresses the main aspects of the prompt.

Coherence: Generally well-organized. Most paragraphs connect logically; some transitions could be stronger.

Vocabulary: Good range of vocabulary. Some repetition acceptable. Generally appropriate word choice.

Grammar: Minor errors present but don't significantly impact understanding. Generally accurate structures.

Score 4–6 (Intermediate)

●

Task fulfillment: Position present but may lack clear development. Some aspects of prompt overlooked.

Coherence: Basic organization visible. Some paragraphs may lack focus or connection to thesis.

Vocabulary: Limited range. Repetitive word choice. May be too formal or too informal.

Grammar: Several errors present. Some affect clarity. Simple sentence structures dominate.

Score 1–3 (Below Intermediate)

●

Task fulfillment: Unclear or missing position. Does not adequately address the prompt.

Coherence: Disorganized. Difficult to follow main argument. Poor paragraph structure.

Vocabulary: Very limited range. Minimal variety in word choice. Inappropriate register.

Grammar: Frequent errors that significantly obscure meaning. Minimal complex structures.

Key insight

A high-scoring essay has a clear thesis, strong evidence, logical organization, and minimal errors. Stay within 200–250 words, use the 5-paragraph structure, and always support claims with specific examples. Quality of argument matters more than length.

Planning

Before writing, plan your three main arguments.

Spend 2 minutes outlining your thesis and three supporting points. This prevents rambling and ensures focus.

Structure

Always use the 5-paragraph formula: intro, 3 body, conclusion.

This gives your essay clear organization examiners expect to see in advanced writing.

Evidence

Support every claim with a specific example.

"Technology improves education" is weak. "Educational software adapts to each student's pace, improving outcomes" is strong.

Evidence

Analyze your evidence; don't just state it.

After each example, explain WHY it supports your thesis. Show critical thinking, not just facts.

Mistakes

Don't introduce new ideas in the conclusion.

The conclusion should restate and summarize, not raise new points. New arguments confuse the reader.

Mistakes

Avoid using "I think" or "in my opinion."

Formal essays state positions as fact: "This approach improves outcomes" not "I think it might help."

Structure

Use transition words to connect paragraphs.

"Furthermore," "In addition," "However," "In contrast" guide readers through your argument.

Planning

Aim for 200–250 words; quality over quantity.

A concise essay with strong arguments scores higher than a padded one with filler sentences.
