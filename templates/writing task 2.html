CELPIP Writing Task 2 Complete Study Guide

What is Writing Task 2?

Respond to a survey or questionnaire. You'll express and explain your opinion on an everyday topic — no special knowledge required.

Time limit

Sample Prompt

A community centre is planning to add new activities for adults. They want to know what kinds of programs people are interested in. Please fill out the survey below and give reasons for your answers.

Common topic types

🏘️ Community & civic
Neighbourhood services, local programs, community centres.
"What facilities would improve your neighbourhood?"
💼 Workplace
Work environment, policies, benefits, scheduling.
"How should your company improve work-life balance?"
🎓 Education
Schools, learning styles, programs, extracurriculars.
"What changes would benefit students most?"
🏪 Consumer & service
Stores, restaurants, public transport, facilities.
"How can this library improve its services?"
⚡ Key difference from Task 1
Task 1 is writing an email to a person. Task 2 is filling out a survey — you respond to multiple questions, each requiring a clear opinion + reasons. There is no recipient to address.

Essay structure

A top-scoring response answers every survey question directly, gives reasons, and includes a personal example or detail where possible.

1

Read all questions first (2 min)

Before typing, read every question in the survey. Underline key words. Decide your position for each. Plan takes 2 minutes — saves 5 minutes of rewriting.

2

Answer question 1 — general opinion

State your opinion clearly and immediately. One sentence for the opinion, two sentences for your reasons. Don't hedge or sit on the fence.

~40 words
3

Answer question 2 — develop your reasoning

Usually asks you to elaborate. Add a personal experience, a specific example, or a "because" chain. This is where mid-range scorers separate from high scorers.

~50 words
4

Answer question 3 — compare / suggest / evaluate

Often asks you to compare options, suggest improvements, or evaluate trade-offs. Give a clear recommendation and explain why the alternative falls short.

~50 words
5

Additional questions (if any)

Some surveys have 4–5 questions. Keep answers concise — don't sacrifice depth on early questions to cover later ones. Balance is key.

~20–30 words each
✓

Review (3 min)

Check: Did you answer every question? Are your ideas clear? Fix grammar and spelling errors. Make sure each answer starts on a new line or is clearly separated.

Preference

Agree / disagree

Nuanced view

Primary reason

Second reason

Causal chain

Result-focused

General example

Hypothetical

How you're scored

CELPIP Writing Task 2 is marked across four criteria. Each is equally weighted.

📋 Content & coherence

  • Answers every survey question
  • Ideas are relevant and focused
  • Logical flow from point to point
  • Adequate development of ideas

📝 Vocabulary

  • Uses varied and precise words
  • Avoids repetition of the same word
  • Correct word choice for context
  • Sophisticated but natural language

🔤 Readability

  • Sentences are clear and easy to follow
  • Variety in sentence length and structure
  • Smooth transitions between ideas
  • Appropriate tone for a survey

✅ Grammar & mechanics

  • Accurate verb tenses throughout
  • Correct subject-verb agreement
  • Proper punctuation and spelling
  • Consistent use of articles (a/an/the)

Pro tips for a top score

Strategies that consistently separate CLB 10+ responses from average ones.

🎯

Answer directly — no preamble

Don't waste words restating the question. Start your answer in sentence one. "In my opinion, yoga classes would be the most valuable addition" is perfect. "This is an interesting question about..." wastes time and word count.

🔄

Use synonyms aggressively

If the prompt uses "beneficial," use "advantageous," "valuable," "worthwhile," and "rewarding" in your answers. Repeating the same word signals a limited vocabulary — one of the four scored criteria.

⏱️

Spend 2 minutes planning

Jot down your opinion and 2 reasons for each question before writing. Planned responses are more coherent and score significantly higher on the Content & Coherence criterion.

📏

Aim for 150–200 words

Under 150 means underdeveloped ideas. Over 200 often introduces errors and unfocused content. The sweet spot is 165–185 words — detailed enough to show range, controlled enough to stay accurate.

🏗️

Mix simple and complex sentences

Don't write all short sentences — or all long ones. Alternate: one complex sentence (with a subordinate clause), one short punchy sentence. This boosts your readability score dramatically.

💡

Include at least one personal example

Graders reward specific, concrete support. "From my personal experience working in an office..." or "When I moved to a new neighbourhood..." immediately makes your response feel authentic and developed.

✅ Do this

  • Answer all survey questions
  • State your opinion in the first sentence
  • Use transition words (furthermore, however)
  • Include a personal example
  • Vary sentence structure
  • Proofread for tense consistency

✗ Avoid this

  • Skip or partially answer a question
  • Use "I think that I think..." filler
  • Repeat the same words throughout
  • Write only 1–2 sentences per question
  • Use slang or overly informal language
  • Leave no time to proofread
